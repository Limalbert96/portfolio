What I Believe

The best customer relationships don't have a seam.

Most SaaS companies split presales and postsales. The SA builds deep context (pain points, architecture, champions) then disappears after the deal closes. A CSM starts over. The customer notices.

I refuse to work that way. I carry the technical relationship from the first whiteboard through onboarding, adoption, and expansion. The trust I build during presales becomes compounding interest in postsales.

The result: my accounts don't churn because they feel forgotten. They expand because I already know where the next opportunity lives.

How this is calculated

The methodology, in the open: this is my model, not a vendor stat. Every technical stakeholder whose relationship doesn't survive the handoff adds about 3% of incremental churn risk (cold relationships are independent failure points, and they compound), capped at a 55% effective rate. The 60% recovery figure is a deliberately conservative estimate of what single owner continuity claws back. The economics underneath are well established: Bain & Company found that a 5% lift in retention can raise profits 25 to 95% (Reichheld, The Loyalty Effect), and both Gainsight and TSIA tie customer success continuity directly to net revenue retention and expansion. Treat the output as directional, not a guarantee. The point still stands: the seam has a price, and I am the person who removes it.
